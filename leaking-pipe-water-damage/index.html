Dealing With Leaking Pipe Water Damage in Revere

You seldom catch a pipe leak the day it begins. Water seeps out behind drywall, at the back of a sink cabinet or under the concrete until a stain, a soft floor or an unexplained water bill gives it away. At that point the drywall and wood nearby may have been soaking for weeks. Our team in Revere finds how far the moisture has spread and dries all of it, not just the part you can see.

Fixing the pipe ends the leak, yet the moisture already inside the wall or floor stays put. This is where our team comes in. We use moisture meters and thermal imaging to map the wet materials, open only what has to be opened, and set air movers and dehumidifiers until readings are back to normal. Because slow leaks give mold time to start, we also check for growth and treat affected surfaces.

First Five Minutes

Before We Arrive

  1. Shut Off the WaterWhen a toilet or sink line is dripping, shut the valve on the supply right at that fixture. Not sure where it is coming from? Close the main shutoff for the whole house.
  2. Stay Clear of ElectricityWater around a switch, outlet or light fixture means the breaker for that part of the house should go off. Leave any wet cord or electrical device alone.
  3. Check the Water MeterMake sure nothing in the house is using water and read the meter. A dial that keeps turning points to a hidden pipe leak, which may be under the slab.
  4. Empty and PhotographEmpty the closet or cabinet by the leak, then photograph the pipe, any stains and any warped material before repairs start or items are discarded.
  5. Have the Area MeteredBefore the opening is sealed, get the surrounding materials checked with a moisture meter. Drywall can feel dry to the touch while the cavity behind it is still damp.
Common Causes

How It Usually Starts

  • Leaks at a sink drain joint or trap, which release a little water with every use.
  • Soil movement and rubbing that wear a hole in a line buried below the slab.
  • Fasteners from remodeling work or wall hangings that nick or pierce a line inside the wall.
  • Cold snaps that freeze piping in unheated spaces, leaving small cracks that leak after the thaw.
  • Hidden joints, soldered or crimped, that fail behind walls and above ceilings.
What Is at Risk

Where the Damage Spreads

Mold Inside the Wall

A dark, damp wall cavity is where mold does best. During a slow leak, the back side of the drywall can be covered well before the painted face shows one spot.

Wet Insulation

Like a sponge, wall insulation takes in water from the pipe and keeps the framing next to it damp. Since a closed wall gives it almost no airflow, soaked insulation is normally removed.

Swollen Cabinets

Sink cabinet bottoms are usually particleboard, which swells and crumbles when it soaks up a leak. Because the board never shrinks back, a long leak often ends with a new cabinet base.

Stained Drywall and Ceilings

Brown water rings and blistered paint show up when a line drips behind a wall or over a ceiling. Drywall that stays wet goes soft, sags and may need to be cut out.

What We Do

How We Handle Leaking Pipe Water Damage

1

Map the Moisture

Every wet area gets marked after a scan of the walls, cabinets and floors with meters and thermal imaging, and that includes places water followed the framing to, some distance from the leak.

2

Make Targeted Openings

Small, planned openings give us access to wet cavities. Insulation, drywall or cabinet pieces beyond saving come out, and moldy material is bagged to keep spores from spreading through the house.

3

Dry the Structure

Air movers and dehumidifiers run until the framing and subfloor reach normal moisture readings, usually 3 to 5 days. We check readings on each visit and adjust the equipment as materials dry.

4

Treat and Document

Affected surfaces get an antimicrobial treatment, and you receive photos along with the moisture logs. The records can help with a claim, and they confirm everything was dry before it was rebuilt.

Insurance

Does Insurance Cover It?

Insurance treats slow leaks differently from sudden ones. Coverage is common for accidental, sudden damage, while a leak that ran for months or weeks is usually not covered and is specifically excluded in some policies. A hidden leak you had no way of knowing about may be handled more favorably than one that was visible and ignored. Read your policy and talk to your agent. So the adjuster can work from solid facts, we provide photos, moisture readings and notes on the leak source.

Pricing

What Drives the Price

  • Whether the damage stayed in one room or spread to others and to the level below.
  • Which finishes and flooring got wet, as some can be saved by drying and some cannot.
  • Mold, if there is any, because containment and removal come first and drying finishes after.
  • What has to be torn out and put back, from drywall and insulation to cabinets and flooring.
