A sewage backup is one of the most hazardous situations a property owner can face. Anyone who comes in contact with contaminated material is exposed to parasites, bacteria and viruses, among them giardia, E. coli, salmonella and hepatitis, all carried in raw sewage and all dangerous. When it happens in Revere, Revere Restoration is available 24/7 with technicians trained for this work, proper protective gear and disinfectants registered with the EPA, everything needed for a safe decontamination.

Sewage damage is classified as Category 3 (black water), the most contaminated and dangerous category of water damage. Because of it, cleanup follows the toughest rules in the trade, which means full protective gear, barriers to contain the area, tearing out any porous material that met sewage and disinfecting all that is left. For Category 3 water damage the reference is IICRC S500, and our crews follow it to bring the building back to safe occupancy.

We clear the standing sewage first, then pull contaminated water out of structural materials, remove carpet, padding, insulation, drywall and any other porous item that was in contact with sewage for proper disposal, disinfect each hard surface, treat with antimicrobial products and finish with structural drying, which prevents secondary mold. We also deodorize the affected area with industrial-grade hydroxyl generators and thermal fogging equipment to eliminate lingering sewage odors.

How to Prevent Sewage Cleanup Problems

A little prevention goes a long way in avoiding expensive restoration emergencies. Here are our expert recommendations:

  • Use drain strainers in showers and sinks to catch hair and debris
  • Toilet paper is the only thing to flush, and that rules out wipes sold as flushable, which still clog
  • In an emergency a plumber needs the cleanout, so learn where yours is and keep it in good shape
  • Keep cooking oil, grease and food scraps out of the drain, where they build into clogs
  • Stormwater from a sump or downspout should never drain into the sewer, since the extra volume invites a backup
  • Protect against municipal sewer backups by having a backwater valve fitted where your line connects

How to Tell You Need Sewage Cleanup

Catching these issues early helps you avoid costly problems and protect your property. Watch for these telltale signs that professional help is needed:

  • Damp patches on a wall or floor that also smell foul
  • Drain openings or basement slabs coated with visible sewage residue
  • A rotten egg or sewer smell from the drains or the lowest floor of the home
  • Toilets or drains that gurgle whenever you run water somewhere else
  • Sluggish drainage at all fixtures, a sign the main line is blocking up
  • A cleanout that weeps sewage, or wet foul patches in the lawn along the sewer path
  • Sewage or dark water backing up through floor drains, toilets or tubs

Why Professional Sewage Cleanup Matters

This is one cleanup you should never take on yourself. Handling raw sewage safely takes specialized training, EPA-registered disinfectants that consumers cannot buy, and personal protective equipment, meaning respirators, Tyvek suits and protective boots and gloves. Hepatitis, gastrointestinal disease and infections of the skin or respiratory system are among the serious illnesses these pathogens cause.

Contaminated materials count as biohazard waste, and disposing of them properly is part of a professional cleanup. Simply placing sewage-contaminated drywall, carpet and insulation in your regular trash is both illegal and hazardous to sanitation workers and the community. A certified restoration company follows proper disposal protocols and works with licensed facilities that accept biohazard waste.
