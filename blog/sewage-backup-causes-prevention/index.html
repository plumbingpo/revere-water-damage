Why Sewage Backups Happen in Revere, MA

Home / Blog / Sewage Backup Causes and Prevention in Revere, MA

For a homeowner, a backed-up sewer line ranks near the top of the list of unpleasant emergencies. The result is extensive damage, an immediate threat to health, and a professional cleanup bill that can reach thousands of dollars whenever raw sewage enters the rooms you live in. Your risk drops significantly when you know what causes backups and take steps to prevent them. Below, Revere Restoration explains what usually causes them for Revere, MA homeowners, plus the practical things you can do to protect your home.

Common Causes of Sewage Backups

A backup happens when wastewater leaving your property cannot reach the septic tank or the municipal sewer system the way it should. The most common causes include: clogged sewer lines from grease, hair, and debris buildup; tree root intrusion into sewer pipes; aging or collapsed sewer pipes; municipal sewer system overloads during heavy rainfall; combined sewer overflow events; and improper items flushed down toilets including wipes, feminine products, and excessive paper.

Tree Root Intrusion

In Revere, many blocked sewer lines turn out to be the work of tree roots. Roots naturally seek moisture, and sewer pipes provide a constant source. Entry happens at connection points, cracks, and joints, and over time the growth packs the pipe, creating the blockages behind many backups. Older sewer lines made of clay tile are especially easy for roots to invade. Schedule a sewer camera inspection every 2 to 3 years so root trouble is found ahead of any backup.

Prevention Strategies

Protecting your Revere home from sewage backups involves several preventive measures: install a backwater valve on your sewer connection to prevent municipal system backflows. Never pour grease or cooking oil down drains. Only flush toilet paper, never wipes, feminine products, or other items. Routine inspection and cleaning of your sewer line should be on the calendar. Deal with a slow drain quickly, before it turns into a full blockage. Find out where your sewer cleanout is so it can be reached in an emergency. It may also be worth looking at sewer line coverage or a backup endorsement for your homeowner's insurance.

Sewage Came Up? Do This First

If you experience a sewage backup, do not attempt cleanup yourself. It carries viruses, bacteria, parasites, and other dangerous pathogens capable of making you seriously ill.
