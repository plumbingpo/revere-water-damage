Saving Water-Damaged Hardwood Floors in Revere, MA

Home / Blog / Water Damage to Hardwood Floors in Revere, MA

Few parts of a house are worth as much as its hardwood flooring, and few are as easily harmed by water. Cupping, crowning, buckling, staining, and mold in the subfloor beneath are all results of water reaching hardwood. Homeowners across Revere, MA who have wood floors benefit from knowing the right response to water and the difference between a floor worth restoring and one that needs replacing.

What Water Does to Wood Floors

Because wood is hygroscopic, it takes in moisture and gives it back as the conditions around it change. When hardwood floors are exposed to excessive water, the wood fibers absorb moisture and expand. This causes cupping (edges rising higher than the center of boards), which is the first visible sign of moisture imbalance. Should the wetting go on, crowning may follow (the bottom dries more slowly than the top, so the center rises above the edges), or buckling (boards pull entirely free of the subfloor). When a floor stays wet a long time, mold can also develop under the boards, on the subfloor, unnoticed until they are pulled.

Can Water-Damaged Hardwood Be Saved?

Saving a wet hardwood floor comes down to the subfloor's condition, the hardwood's type and finish, the length of time it stayed wet, the amount of water, and how soon professional drying starts. If the water was clean and a professional begins drying inside of 24 to 48 hours, the hardwood is salvageable in many cases. The key is controlled, gradual drying that allows the wood to release moisture evenly without cracking or splitting.

Professional Hardwood Floor Drying

Drying hardwood is not done the way ordinary structural drying is, and it calls for its own techniques. Drying mats or panels are placed on the hardwood surface to create controlled drying conditions that pull moisture from the wood evenly. Where there is a crawl space, drying equipment can work on the subfloor from below as well. Slow and even is the rule: rush it and the surface dries ahead of the core, leaving permanent splits, cracks, and cupping.

Signs the Floor Cannot Be Saved

Replacement is usually the answer if the subfloor is compromised or has mold on it, the boards have buckled or delaminated extensively, sewage, floodwater, or other contaminated water touched the wood, or the floor stayed wet longer than 48 to 72 hours.
